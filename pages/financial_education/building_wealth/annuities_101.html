Annuities 101

Annuities are insurance contracts designed to turn savings into reliable, long-term income. They can help manage longevity risk — the risk of outliving your money — when used thoughtfully within a broader plan.

What Is an Annuity?

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You pay money into the contract (either all at once or over time), and in return the insurer promises:

  • To grow your money on a tax-deferred basis while it stays in the annuity
  • To provide a stream of income in the future — sometimes for life
  • To offer options for beneficiaries if you pass away (depending on the contract)

Annuities are not bank accounts and are typically intended as long-term retirement products. The guarantees depend on the claims-paying ability of the insurer, and some contracts involve investment risk.

Where Annuities Fit in a Long-Term Plan

At V & V Advisors, we teach that annuities are tools, not magic solutions. They may be helpful when you:

  • Want a portion of your retirement income to be reliable and predictable
  • Are concerned about outliving your savings (longevity risk)
  • Have already built a foundation: emergency fund, manageable debt, and basic retirement savings
  • Value tax-deferred growth and are comfortable with money being tied up for many years

Research from regulators and consumer agencies highlights that annuities can play a role in retirement security when chosen carefully — especially for those who want income they cannot outlive.

Core Types of Annuities

There are two big questions with annuities: When does income start? and How does the money grow? The table below combines both.

Type When Income Starts How It Grows Typical Use
Immediate Income Annuity Soon after a lump-sum deposit (often within 12 months) Fixed payments based on interest rates, age, and payout option Turning a portion of savings into guaranteed monthly income in retirement
Deferred Fixed Annuity Later, when you choose to start income Grows at a set interest rate or declared rate by the insurer Tax-deferred growth with principal protection (subject to the insurer’s guarantees)
Fixed Indexed Annuity Later, when you elect income or add an income rider Interest credits tied to a market index (e.g., S&P 500) with caps/floors — you do not own the index itself A blend of downside protection with potential for higher credited interest than a traditional fixed rate
Variable Annuity Later, via withdrawals or annuitization Invested in underlying subaccounts similar to mutual funds — value can go up or down Tax-deferred investing with optional guarantees, but includes market risk and higher fees

*Descriptions based on guidance from the U.S. SEC, FINRA, and NAIC. Specific features vary by insurer and contract.

How Annuities Grow Over Time

While your money stays inside an annuity, it generally grows tax-deferred — you don’t pay taxes until you take the money out. How it grows depends on the contract:

  • Fixed: Earns a declared or guaranteed minimum interest rate.
  • Indexed: Credits interest using a formula based on a market index (with caps, spreads, or participation rates).
  • Variable: Grows or shrinks based on investment performance of underlying subaccounts.

Tax deferral can amplify long-term growth, but it also means withdrawals may be taxed as ordinary income — and additional penalties may apply for early withdrawals (often before age 59½).

Benefits and Trade-Offs

Like any financial tool, annuities involve both advantages and important trade-offs to review carefully.

Potential Benefits Key Trade-Offs / Risks
Option for income you cannot outlive Surrender charges for withdrawing too much or too early
Tax-deferred growth while money stays in the contract Possible market risk (especially with variable annuities)
Helps manage longevity and sequence-of-returns risk Fees (mortality & expense charges, rider fees, investment expenses)
Some contracts offer death benefits or income riders Money may be less liquid than other investment accounts

This page is educational and does not recommend any specific product. Always review a contract’s disclosure, fees, and surrender schedule before deciding.

Fees, Riders & Surrender Periods

Regulators such as FINRA and NAIC emphasize that understanding costs is critical before buying an annuity. Common cost areas include:

  • Surrender charges: Fees if you withdraw more than the free amount during the surrender period.
  • Mortality & expense (M&E) fees: Common in variable annuities, covering insurance guarantees and administrative costs.
  • Rider fees: Extra cost for features like guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefits or enhanced death benefits.
  • Investment expenses: Underlying fund or subaccount costs in variable annuities.

Riders can add valuable guarantees, but they increase fees and complexity. At V & V Advisors, we focus on whether riders genuinely support your goals, not just add features.

Is an Annuity Right for Me?

Use this simple reflection checklist as a starting point before discussing specifics with a licensed professional.

Question Yes No / Unsure
Do I already have an emergency fund and manageable debt?
Is my primary goal reliable long-term income in retirement?
Am I comfortable locking money up for many years to get these benefits?
Have I reviewed the fees, surrender schedule, and insurer ratings?
Have I compared this annuity to other retirement tools (401(k), IRA, etc.)?

If many of your answers fall under “No / Unsure,” it may be helpful to focus first on foundations (budgeting, emergency savings, retirement accounts) before adding annuities.
