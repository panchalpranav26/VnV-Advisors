Fixed vs. Indexed vs. Variable

Three common ways money can grow — each with a different balance of safety, risk, and long-term potential. Understanding these differences helps you make confident, informed decisions for your financial future.

Three Ways Money Can Grow

When building long-term wealth, most strategies fall into one of three growth types: fixed, indexed, or variable. Each reacts differently to markets, offers different protections, and supports different goals.

  • Fixed: Stability, guarantees, and predictable growth
  • Indexed: Growth linked to an index with downside protection
  • Variable: Full market participation with higher risk

Side-by-Side Comparison

Feature Fixed Indexed Variable
Risk Level Low Low–Moderate High
Market Exposure No Linked (no direct investment) Yes — fully invested
Protection Against Loss Yes — guaranteed rate Yes — 0% or floor No — value fluctuates
Growth Potential Moderate Moderate–High High (with volatility)

Fixed Growth

Fixed accounts offer a guaranteed interest rate. They do not change with the market, making them predictable and stable. According to the SEC, fixed accounts are best suited for conservative savers or those nearing retirement.

  • Guaranteed interest rate
  • Not affected by market downturns
  • Lower long-term growth potential
  • Minimal or no fees

Common examples: CDs, Fixed Annuities, Fixed Accounts in UL or IUL policies

Indexed Growth

Indexed strategies are tied to a market index (such as the S&P 500®) but do not directly invest in it. According to NAIC, indexed products allow potential growth with downside protection.

  • 0% floor protects against market loss
  • Growth linked to index performance
  • May include caps, spreads, or participation rates
  • Designed for balanced, steady long-term growth

Common examples: Indexed Annuities, Indexed Universal Life (IUL) crediting strategies

Variable Growth

Variable accounts directly invest in the market through funds or subaccounts. FINRA identifies variable products as offering the highest potential return — but also the highest risk.

  • No downside protection — full market exposure
  • Highest long-term return potential
  • Greater volatility and short-term fluctuation
  • Typically higher fees

Common examples: Mutual funds, Variable Universal Life (VUL), Variable Annuities

The Risk Spectrum

Understanding the differences between Fixed, Indexed, and Variable annuities helps families choose the right balance of stability, growth, and risk. This spectrum shows where each product sits based on volatility, predictability, and exposure to market movement.

Fixed Annuities

Lowest risk. Guaranteed interest rate. No market exposure.

Indexed Annuities

Moderate risk. Linked to a market index with downside protection.

Variable Annuities

Highest risk. Fully market-exposed. Gains and losses vary.

*Risk levels based on market exposure, volatility, and principal protection. Sources: FINRA, SEC Investor Education, NAIC Annuity Guide.

Which One Should You Choose?

The “best” option depends on your timeline, risk tolerance, current financial foundation, and long-term goals.

Goal Best Fit
Protect principal Fixed or Indexed
Balance growth & safety Indexed
Maximize long-term returns Variable
Shorter time horizon Fixed
Long investment timeline (10–20+ yrs) Indexed or Variable
