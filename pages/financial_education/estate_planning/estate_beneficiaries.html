Beneficiary Designations

One of the simplest — but most important — ways to protect your family. Beneficiary designations ensure your assets pass directly and swiftly to the people you choose.

What Are Beneficiary Designations?

A beneficiary is the person or entity you choose to receive certain financial assets when you pass away. Beneficiary designations override wills and trusts — meaning the money goes directly to the person listed on the account.

This applies to accounts such as:

  • Life insurance policies
  • Retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, Roth IRA)
  • Annuities
  • HSAs and some investment accounts

According to the CFPB, properly set beneficiary designations can make asset transfers faster, simpler, and more cost-effective for families.

Why Beneficiary Designations Matter

For most families, beneficiary designations are one of the most important parts of estate planning. FINRA highlights that outdated or missing beneficiary selections can lead to delays, disputes, or assets going to the wrong person.

  • Avoids probate — assets transfer directly and quickly
  • Protects your family's privacy — records do not become public
  • Speeds up access — especially important for surviving spouses and children
  • Ensures your wishes are followed exactly

Types of Beneficiaries

Primary Beneficiary

The main person(s) who will receive the assets. Most families choose a spouse, child, or trusted relative.

Contingent Beneficiary

The backup. This person receives the asset only if the primary beneficiary is unable or unwilling to receive it.

Per Stirpes vs. Per Capita

Distribution instructions specifying whether your beneficiary’s children inherit their share if they pass away before you.

Trust or Entity

Some families choose to name a trust, charity, or estate — depending on goals and guidance from licensed professionals.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Not Updating After Major Life Events

Marriage, divorce, new children — outdated beneficiaries cause the most real-world issues.

❌ Only Listing One Person

Without contingents, assets may get stuck in probate.

❌ Naming Minors Directly

Minors cannot legally receive funds — courts may need to appoint a guardian.

❌ Not Matching Your Will or Trust

Beneficiary designations override estate documents.
