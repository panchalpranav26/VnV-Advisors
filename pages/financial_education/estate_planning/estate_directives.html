Advance Directives

Advance Directives ensure *your wishes are honored* during medical situations when you cannot speak for yourself. They protect your dignity, your family, and your future — long before you ever need them.

What Are Advance Directives?

Advance Directives are legal documents that outline your medical care preferences if you become unable to communicate. According to the National Institute on Aging, these directives ensure doctors and family members know exactly what you want.

In California, Advance Directives are governed by the Health Care Decisions Act, which covers both your treatment wishes and who can legally make care decisions on your behalf.

  • Your medical treatment preferences
  • Who you trust to make decisions
  • Your wishes during emergencies or end-of-life care
  • Instructions to reduce family stress or conflict

Types of Advance Directives

A complete Advance Directive typically includes two core documents, each serving a different purpose.

1. Living Will

A Living Will describes which medical treatments you want — or do not want — in certain situations.

  • Life support preferences
  • Resuscitation (DNR/DNI)
  • Treatments for severe illness or coma
  • Pain management preferences

2. Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care

This names the person (“agent”) who can legally make medical decisions on your behalf. AARP research shows families with appointed agents experience less stress and fewer disputes.

  • You choose someone who understands your values
  • Agents can speak to doctors when you cannot
  • Covers emergency and long-term situations
  • Stronger than a verbal request

Why Advance Directives Matter

Advance Directives protect more than medical decisions — they protect families from emotional burden.

  • Prevents family conflict or guesswork
  • Ensures medical care aligns with your beliefs
  • Reduces stress during emergencies
  • Speeds up medical response because your preferences are documented
  • Legally empowers your chosen decision-maker

Studies from the NIH show individuals with Advance Directives receive care that closely aligns with their values — compared to those without documented instructions.

When Are Advance Directives Used?

Emergency Situations

Accidents, sudden illness, or conditions that affect consciousness.

Serious or Chronic Illness

Heart disease, cancer, neurological conditions, or degenerative diseases.

Surgery or Hospitalization

If sedation, anesthesia, or potential complications may limit communication.

End-of-Life Situations

Guides compassionate care that respects your dignity and values.

How to Create an Advance Directive

In most states, including California, creating an Advance Directive is simple and free.

  1. Download your state’s Advance Directive form
  2. Choose your health care agent
  3. Document your medical treatment preferences
  4. Sign with required witnesses or a notary
  5. Share copies with family, your agent, and your doctor
  6. Store copies in a safe but accessible place

California’s official Advance Directive form is available through the Attorney General’s Office.
