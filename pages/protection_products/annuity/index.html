Annuity Products

Annuities are long-term insurance products that can turn savings into predictable income and help protect what you’ve built. At V & V Advisors, we focus on helping families understand when an annuity may — or may not — fit into a protection-focused financial plan.

What Is an Annuity?

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You pay in money (either over time or as a lump sum), and in return the insurer agrees to provide:

  • Growth on your contributions (fixed, indexed, or market-linked)
  • Tax-deferred accumulation while your money stays in the annuity
  • Options for guaranteed income for a set period or for life

Regulatory bodies such as the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), FINRA, and the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) emphasize that annuities are long-term commitments and should be matched carefully to your goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance.

How Annuities Fit Into a Protection Plan

Annuities are not “grow as fast as possible” products — they are designed to support income stability, longevity risk, and principal protection (depending on type).

  • Income you can’t outlive — certain annuity options can pay as long as you live.
  • Transition from saving to drawing — bridge from working years to retirement income.
  • Protection from outliving savings — helps manage “will my money last?” risk.
  • Optional guarantees — riders may add income guarantees or legacy features (with extra cost).

Important: Annuities are insurance products, not bank deposits. Guarantees are based on the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurance company. They may include fees, surrender charges, and restrictions that should be reviewed carefully.

Annuity Product Overview

At V & V Advisors, we group annuity options into three practical categories to help families understand trade-offs between protection, growth potential, and flexibility.

Traditional Annuities

Focus on stability and predictability. These products typically provide a guaranteed interest rate or guaranteed income, making them easier to understand.

  • Fixed annuities with guaranteed interest
  • Multi-year guaranteed rate (MYGA) annuities
  • Immediate annuities that start income soon

Growth-Oriented Annuities

Designed to balance growth potential with downside limits or protections. Often used when families want long-term accumulation plus future income options.

  • Fixed indexed annuities (growth tied to an index with limits)
  • Some variable or registered annuity designs (investment-linked)
  • Options for riders that guarantee future income based on growth

Specialized Annuities

Target specific needs such as longevity, legacy, or health-related costs. These can be layered on top of a retirement plan to solve very focused risks.

  • Longevity or deferred income annuities (income later in life)
  • Annuities with long-term care or chronic illness benefits
  • Legacy-focused structures for beneficiaries

Questions to Ask Before Choosing an Annuity

Before moving forward with any annuity, regulators such as FINRA and the SEC encourage consumers to ask clear, direct questions.

  • Do I understand how this annuity grows (fixed rate, index crediting, or investments)?
  • What fees, surrender charges, or rider costs apply — and for how long?
  • When and how can I access my money without penalties?
  • What guarantees are backed by the insurance company, and what risks do I still carry?
  • How does this annuity complement my other retirement accounts and income sources?

Common Questions About Annuities

Are annuities right for everyone?

No. Annuities are long-term products and may be more appropriate for individuals who have a longer time horizon, want income stability, and understand the trade-offs in liquidity and fees. They are not a fit for all situations.

What is the main difference between “traditional” and “growth-oriented” annuities?

Traditional annuities generally prioritize guarantees and stability (fixed rates, predictable income). Growth-oriented annuities seek higher potential returns by tying growth to indexes or investments, often with more complexity and risk.

How are annuities regulated?

Insurance aspects are regulated at the state level (e.g., the California Department of Insurance and NAIC), while certain investment-linked annuities are also overseen by federal regulators like the SEC and FINRA.

Can an annuity be part of my estate or legacy plan?

Yes. Many annuities allow you to name beneficiaries and may offer riders or features that support legacy goals. This should be coordinated with your broader estate planning (wills, trusts, and beneficiary designations).
